# Outline

Overall Goal of Project: First Generation College Students are the Underrepresented Majority and we Should Create and Share Tech Resources for Them

Part I: What are First Generation College Students

This part is where I'm going to be talking about the demographics and interesting statistics about first-generation college students. Specifically, I want to highlight statistics that would make my audience surprised. For example, I'll talk about the graduation rates for first-generation college students versus the graduation rate for continuing-generation college students. I will start off this part by talking about what a first-generation college student is because I feel like that is a point of confusion for people who don't work or have peers who are first-gen. I'll give almost a dictionary definition of what it is. Specifically for my project, I'm focusing on first-generation college students in the United States, and I'm looking at data that is post-2020, at least. I'm also focusing stronger on undergraduate students, and I'll also put stats to show how many first-generation college students make up the undergraduate student population of the United States. I'll also include demographic information, such as the percentage of students that are Asian, Black, Latino, etc.

Part II: Differences in Career Outcomes Between First-Gen & Continuing-Gen Students

For this part of the project, I'm going to focus specifically on the gap that exists between first-generation college students and continuing-generation students, specifically when it comes to degree outcomes and career outcomes. I'll make a comparison of how many first-generation college students earn a bachelor's degree versus how many continuing-generation college students earn a bachelor's degree. In that specific discussion, I'll also look at how the dropout rates for college differ, and I'll also start to look at income gaps. Specifically, when it comes to career access gaps, I'm going to look at data that shows:

- the percent of first-generation college students that were using the career services at their university
- how internship placement was different for them compared to continuing generation students
- if their internship pay was different as well

Part III: Tools to Help (CTA)

This is the part where I'm going to talk about why I created my resource called the Layan Library. The Layan Library is a hub of resources for first-generation college students. It's going to include a section that is a database filled with organizations that help first-generation college students with certain aspects, such as:

- internship placement
- career prep
- networking
- mentorship
- scholarships

My call to action in this case addresses three different types of people:
- For the first-generation college student, I would encourage them to sign up for the waitlist of my tool and share it with their peers.
- If they're educators, I would encourage them to share my tool with their students.
- If they're technological builders, I would encourage them to also create tools that increase information accessibility.
